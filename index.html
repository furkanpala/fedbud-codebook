An interactive course

One large model, cut into sub-networks that small clients train together

Hospitals and small companies cannot train a large language model alone, and they must keep their data private. This course explains, step by step, how federated budgeted decomposition (FedBUD) gives each of them a sub-network, a part of one large model that fits the computation the client can spend, its budget, and how they train these sub-networks together.

How to use this course

Each lesson explains one idea in plain words, lets you try it, asks one to three questions, and ends with a short summary. Try to answer each question before you press Show the result.

Everything runs in your browser. The small models train on your own computer in a few seconds, and the Python cells run through Pyodide, a version of Python for the browser. No data leave your computer. The numbers that the toy models produce are your own results, not results of the paper.

You need a recent browser and an internet connection to load the course. Lessons 1 to 3 work with any browser. Lessons with training work best on a laptop or desktop computer.

About this course

The course follows the paper on federated budgeted decomposition (FedBUD). Every statement about the method and its results comes from the paper.
