Lesson 1

The problem: large models, small clients

Large language models need a lot of computation and data to train. Hospitals and small companies have little of both, and they must keep their data private.

The idea

A large language model (LLM) is a network that reads and writes text. It learns from examples by changing its parameters, the numbers inside the network. A larger model has more parameters, so it needs more memory to store them and more computation to use them.

Think of a district hospital that wants a model to find drug names in its clinical notes. Such an organization, a client, owns one graphics card and a few thousand notes. The client cannot train a large model alone. A model of a few billion parameters does not fit on its graphics card, and even the half-billion model of the paper costs computation for every token it reads, more than the clinic can spend, while its few thousand notes are too few to train it well. It also must not send its notes to a server outside the hospital, because the notes belong to its patients.

The FedBUD paper starts from Qwen2.5-0.5B, a language model with about half a billion parameters. To fine-tune a model is to continue training it on the client's own notes. Use the slider below to see how memory grows with model size, against the memory of one graphics card.

Questions

Question 1

A clinic has one graphics card with 16 GB of memory. With the rule of 16 bytes per parameter, what is the largest model it can fine-tune in full? Give the size in billions of parameters.

About 1 billion parameters, because 16 GB divided by 16 bytes per parameter is 1 billion. The clinic can fine-tune the half-billion model of the paper, but not a model of 7 billion parameters. Memory is only the first limit. Lesson 3 shows the second, the computation each token costs.

Question 2

Why can the clinic not send its notes to a large hospital that owns more graphics cards?

The second answer. Privacy rules keep patient records inside the clinic. So the clinic needs a way to learn from other clients without moving its data. Lesson 2 shows such a way.

Summary

    
A client such as a clinic or a small company owns little memory, little computation and little data, and it must keep its data private. Even a model of a few billion parameters is too large for it to fine-tune alone. The rest of this course shows how FedBUD lets such clients train small parts of one large model together, without sharing their data.
