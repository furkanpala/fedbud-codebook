Lesson 10

What the paper found

Every number in this lesson comes from the paper. The lesson ends with a quiz on the whole course.

The experiments

The paper starts from Qwen2.5-0.5B, a language model with about half a billion parameters, and gives each client a budget of 0.6 of the cost of the blocks it can cut. With the head, which no method can cut, this is between 0.68 and 0.71 of the full model's cost per token. The paper runs six experiments.

  • CoNLL-2003: find the names of people, places and organizations in news text. Three clients.
  • SQuAD: answer a question with a span of a given passage. Three clients.
  • AG News: sort news articles by topic. Three clients.
  • XSum: summarize a news article in one sentence. Three clients.
  • SAMSum: summarize a chat conversation. Three clients.
  • Multi-task: four clients, each with a different task.

In every experiment the clients hold unequal shares of the data with different mixes of labels, topics or tasks. The paper compares FedBUD with published methods that cut a model to the same budget, with a client that trains alone, and with the full model fine-tuned on each client's data.

The findings

First, at the same cost per token, the sub-networks that FedBUD trains together scored above the best published method that cuts a model, on six of six datasets. The published methods ran once each, so this comparison has no statistical test.

Second, training together beat each client training alone on six of six datasets in the mean over three runs. Across the datasets the gain is significant, and it is significant on its own on three of them, SQuAD, SAMSum and the multi-task experiment.

Third, aligned averaging scored higher than averaging position by position on five of six datasets in one series of runs, but it raised the loss of the multi-task experiment in both series. Fourth, the allocator's sub-networks beat random sub-networks on five of six datasets and tied on the sixth, while a hand-designed rule did better than the allocator on four of six. Fifth, sharing one sub-network or the importance scores across clients, when the validation data choose it, helped significantly on CoNLL and XSum. Finally, at 0.68 to 0.71 of the cost, the sub-networks scored below the full model fine-tuned on each client's data on all six datasets. That gap is the price of the budget.

Summary of the course

Clients such as hospitals and small companies cannot train a large language model alone, and they must keep their data private. FedBUD reads the large model as a graph, scores its parts on each client's data, and lets a learned allocator cut one sub-network per client within the client's budget. The clients then train their sub-networks together with aligned averaging, without sharing data. In the paper, this beat the published methods that cut a model to the same budget and beat training alone on all six datasets, while staying below the full model that the clients could not afford. The paper names its limits plainly. It tested one budget, one model of half a billion parameters, and three or four clients.
